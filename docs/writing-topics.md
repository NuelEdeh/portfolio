## Professional, reframed from lived experience
- **They know the recommended action. They cannot take it.** — the cyber poverty line as a general condition, not a security one. The distance between knowing what to do and having the leadership, budget, or capacity to do it. Strongest under-explored idea in *Who Applies the Brakes*.
- **Funding the boring thing** — reliability, migrations, internal tooling: invisible when they work, career-defining when they fail. Why organisations systematically under-invest in work with no demo.
- **Selling confidence, not features** — the CISO-anxiety insight, generalized into a positioning philosophy.
- **PM as translator** — turning resolver degradation / incidents into narratives execs can act on.
- **What I look for now** — the team/problem you want next; doubles as a signal to people reading the site.
- **The gated rollout** — shipping two ways (straight to the process engine vs. gated to one customer); trust at scale.
- **The invisible half of leadership** — the introspective work leaders never show, so it never gets modelled or practised. Expands a section of *Ship Fast*.

## Where-you're-at / the AI moment
- **The traffic was planned for humans** — a person makes a few logins and a handful of clicks; their agent makes thousands. Every system downstream was capacity-planned for the human number, and nobody has re-planned. Deliberately cut from *Who Applies the Brakes* so it could have its own run. *(next pick — raw material already parked)*
- **Does the machine want autonomy too?** — the question *What My Cat Taught Me About Control* raised and refused to answer. If autonomy is control's twin in humans, what are we handing to systems we don't understand?
- **Intelligence is rentable; the heat is not** — expand the closing thesis of the chem-eng piece into its own essay.
- **An AI-native PM in an AI-anxious market** — field notes from building agents *and* job-hunting while the ground shifts.
- **What agentic AI can't do** — initiative, intention, the spark; the body in the room that feels whether it's working.
- **The PM in the age of cheap process** — if the process is rentable, the job becomes choosing *which* process to start.

## A "Principles" series (reusable format)
Each is short: **Principle / Elaboration / Example.** Fast to write, builds a body of work.
- Roadmaps are best guesses *(done — "Held With Confidence")*
- Reliability is a process, not a feature *(largely absorbed by "Who Applies the Brakes")*
- Time-to-value is existential (from Neat)
- Start in the petri dish (MVP's only job is to gather data)
- Listen to the heat (innovation as courage before validation)
- Hold twenty percent (the reliability slice, and why 15 is the floor)
- A no needs a next *(largely absorbed by "Liked or Trusted")*
- Coverage is not presence (being everywhere and landing nowhere)
- Why is this coming up right now (the discovery question; the story is not the reason)

## Format ideas
- **Long-form flagship** (~800–1100 words) — the essays.
- **Short paste-ables** — LinkedIn-sized teasers that link back to the full piece (we drafted one for the chem-eng essay).
- **Principles** — bite-size, structured, frequent.
